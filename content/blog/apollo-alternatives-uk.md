## Why UK teams look beyond Apollo

Apollo.io is genuinely good at what it was built for: a very large, US-centric B2B database bolted to a capable sequencing engine, with a free tier that is honestly useful for testing. Per-seat pricing looks cheap on paper. None of that is in dispute.

The complaints that recur in public reviews cluster around four themes — and they matter more the further your market is from the US:

- **Data accuracy outside the US.** Reviewers on G2 and similar sites frequently report that EMEA and small-company records are noticeably weaker than US enterprise records: stale job titles, people who left years ago, wrong or catch-all emails. For a UK team prospecting UK SMEs — the segment where records go stale fastest — that means bounces and wasted credits.
- **Billing and account complaints.** Apollo's Trustpilot rating has hovered around the high-2s, with a visible share of reviews describing disputed renewals, unexpected charges and account suspensions. We can't verify individual stories, but the pattern is public enough that you should read the renewal terms carefully.
- **Credit overages.** The headline per-seat price rarely reflects real spend. Export, enrichment and add-on credits mean teams commonly report effective costs in the $150–400 per user per month range — several multiples of the sticker price.
- **Complexity.** Apollo does prospecting, enrichment, sequencing, dialling, deals and intent. Reviewers often describe the UI as overwhelming for small teams who just want to find people and email them.

To be fair: if you sell into US mid-market and enterprise, Apollo's database depth is hard to beat at the price. This article is for teams whose pipeline looks different — UK-first, SME-heavy, and tired of paying in dollars for data that bounces.

## Database vs engagement tool: know what you're replacing

Apollo is two products in one: a **contact database** (find people, get emails) and an **engagement platform** (sequences, mailboxes, tracking). Most alternatives are one or the other, so decide which half you're actually replacing:

- **Database-first tools** (Cognism, Hunter.io) give you contacts but expect you to send from something else.
- **Engagement-first tools** (Instantly, Smartlead, Lemlist, Saleshandy) send brilliantly but assume you bring your own list.
- **Both-in-one tools** (Velox House, and Apollo itself) find the people and run the outreach.

Pairing works fine — Hunter for emails plus Smartlead for sending is a common combination — but you'll manage two subscriptions and a CSV export between them. If that sounds tedious, shortlist the all-in-one options first. Our [best cold email software guide](/blog/best-cold-email-software-2026/) covers the sending side in more depth.

## UK-specific considerations before you switch

Whichever tool you pick, a few things matter specifically for UK teams:

- **PECR, not just GDPR.** UK cold email to corporate addresses is lawful under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, but sole traders and partnerships are treated differently, and you must identify yourself and offer opt-out. Our guide to [whether cold email is legal in the UK](/blog/is-cold-email-legal-uk/) covers the detail.
- **GBP billing.** Dollar-priced tools add FX fees and make renewals unpredictable. Only Velox House and Cognism on this list invoice in pounds.
- **VAT invoicing.** US vendors don't always issue VAT-compliant invoices cleanly; if you reclaim VAT, check how each handles UK VAT (reverse charge on US services) before your accountant has to.
- **Support hours.** US-headquartered tools often run support on Pacific time — a 9am problem in London may not get a reply until mid-afternoon.
- **Sending limits still apply.** Whatever you choose, Google and Microsoft mailbox limits and the Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, spam rate under 0.3%) don't change — see [how many cold emails you can send per day](/blog/how-many-cold-emails-per-day/).

Apollo's database is strongest for US mid-market and enterprise contacts. Public reviews frequently report weaker accuracy for EMEA and small-company records — stale titles and wrong emails — which matters if your pipeline is UK SMEs. It isn't "bad" universally; it's optimised for a different market than most UK teams sell into.

Saleshandy is a budget sending tool; Velox House also finds and SMTP-verifies contacts, so compare total cost including where your data comes from, not just the sending licence.

### Which Apollo alternative has the best UK and European data?

Cognism is widely regarded as the leader for UK/EU coverage, including phone-verified mobiles, but it's priced for enterprise annual contracts. For self-serve budgets, Velox House sources UK-relevant contacts and SMTP-verifies every one before import, so you don't pay for addresses that bounce.

Only if the tool covers both halves of Apollo: the contact database and the sending engine. Velox House does both (find, verify, write, send); most others cover one half, so you'd pair a data tool like Hunter with a sender like Smartlead or Instantly.

### Do Apollo alternatives handle LinkedIn outreach too?
